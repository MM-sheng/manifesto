# The New Futurist Manifesto

[中文版](README.zh-CN.md)

The future does not arrive on its own. Someone makes it.

Today most of it is decided inside a handful of companies. They decide which tools you get, where your data goes, what you may and may not do. Most people wait for the future to be finished and handed to them.

We don't wait.

We believe the future belongs to the people willing to invent it. One person, one computer and one weekend are enough to make something that did not exist before. PGP was written by one person. Bitcoin began as nine pages and a small program. The things that change the world start small.

We don't ask permission. If something should exist, we make it.

We believe knowledge should move freely. We show what we make, so others can learn from it, change it and take it further.

We stand with the individual. A hundred years ago the first Futurists worshipped the machine and the power behind it. The new futurism puts the machine in the individual's hands. A good tool leaves the person using it more capable and more independent, and less dependent on any platform.

We value ideas as much as code. Thinking a problem all the way through, and writing it down so someone else can carry it forward, is also invention.

Plenty of people talk about the future. Few make it. We are looking for the few.

None of this exists yet. There is this manifesto and an empty room. If you are making something, however small or rough, bring it, and help decide what this becomes.

We are looking for people inventing the future. We call this the New Futurism. If that's you, come.

— Mario sheng, October 2026

## Sign it

If this describes you, add your name to [SIGNATORIES.md](SIGNATORIES.md) with a pull request: your name or handle, and one line about what you are making.

Not a git user? Open an issue with the same two things and you will be added.

## Show what you are making

Post it in [Discussions](../../discussions) under **Show and tell**. A tool, an experiment, a design, an essay that works a problem through. Small and rough is fine.
